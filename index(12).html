Synopsis

Revolution in Aging Research

Across California — from Silicon Valley garages to cutting-edge university labs — scientists are working on a prominent question: can we slow down, stop, or even reverse aging?

We follow the researchers, entrepreneurs, and dreamers racing to crack this problem. They're testing every approach from regenerating organs to using AI to understand biological data. Along the way, we meet living creatures that seem to have figured it out already — species that simply don't age.

Told through the eyes of biologists and filmmakers, this is a scientific journey which reshape the future, the human body, everyday life, and the experience of being human.

If we could intervene in aging itself — would it change what it means to be alive?

Artistic Approach

An immersive documentary

The first documentary to capture pioneering longevity experiments from the inside. We are combining rare scientific access with an artistic vision that transforms biology into a cinematic experience.

The aesthetic oscillates between luminescent cellular forms, the rhythms of ageless creatures, and the precision of human biotechnology.

01

Real-Time experiments

Groundbreaking science filmed as it unfolds — cryopreservation, parabiosis, genome editing — captured on camera for the first time. No reconstructions. Science as high-stakes action.

02

Science as art

Fluorescent cells, translucent embryos, luminescent organisms. Complex biology transformed into poetic imagery — Koyaanisqatsi meets the science of aging. A meditative visual experience.

03

Leading scientists

Embedded with foremost gerontologists at Stanford, UCSF, and beyond. Access secured over years to labs that have never opened their doors to a film crew.

Our Mission

Redefine aging for society

We believe the science of longevity is one of the defining stories of our time — yet it remains largely invisible to the public. Our mission is to bring this field out of the laboratory: to spark an honest, informed conversation. What does it mean to extend human life? Who gets access to these technologies?

Through art, science, and storytelling, Longevity aims to become a bridge between researchers and the public — making the complexity of aging science accessible without simplifying it, and raising the ethical and political questions that this revolution demands.

"A kidney loses color, becomes translucent, suspended between states neither alive nor dead. Then it is transplanted back, regains color, fills with blood, and returns to life."
